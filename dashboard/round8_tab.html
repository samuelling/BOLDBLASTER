Experiment 7 · Round 8

Does the game matter? Three real games against the raycaster

What we did

Every earlier round used BOLDBLASTER's own raycaster. Here the stimulus is three of our real games, each played by a bot for twelve 5-minute runs and shown on the same 24 × 18° screen at 192 × 144 px:

  • Betamax: the Mayflower Beach battle, played forward with no rewind and no taking bodies, rendered headless in WebGL.
  • Brick Ninja: the ten side-scrolling worlds, with jumping, stars and sword.
  • Animal Sanctuary: the five wild zones, walking up to creatures and playing the befriending timing game.

The game's own logs stand in for the raycaster's NPCs, coins and shots, so every model fits exactly as before. Gaze targets are enemies or creatures on screen, items are soldiers, studs or creatures, and outcomes are bursts, stars or catch presses. Each game is compared with the raycaster at a matched 30 and 60 minutes.

RESULT

How each game feeds these models

Numerosity counts the game's natural items: coins (raycaster), enemies within 60 m (Betamax), studs (Brick Ninja) and creatures on screen (Animal Sanctuary). Self-motion uses forward speed and turn rate in the 3D games. In the 2D games horizontal camera scroll stands in for turning and forward motion is zero, so the expansion weight has nothing to fit. Reward prediction error is each shot's outcome (a burst, a star, a catch press) minus its expected hit probability, from a logistic fit per game. Spatiotemporal (CST) fits take each game's own joint V1 fit as plug-in tuning.

Caveats

* Round 7 ran the raycaster's CST at 20, 40 and 60 minutes, so its 30-minute value is the mean of the 20- and 40-minute fits.

The raycaster's procedural textures put more energy at high spatial frequency. Betamax falls off like a natural photograph. The pixel-art games are dominated by low frequencies and hard pixel edges, which is part of why SF tuning is harder to pin down from Animal Sanctuary.

How each bot plays

Betamax's bot defends the beach as a Cape Cod soldier, snapping onto Texans with a reaction delay and firing bursts, and carries on as the next man up when it falls. Brick Ninja's bot runs right, jumps gaps and walls, throws aimed stars and swings its sword up close. Animal Sanctuary's bot walks to the nearest creature and presses during the catch bar with human-like timing error, or picks berries when nothing is near. All three play at 10 frames per second of game time, rendered by the game itself.
